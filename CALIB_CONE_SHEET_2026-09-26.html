Before going out

  1. Weather: dry, bright overcast, little wind. In sun, work within two to three hours of midday (short shadows, no glare). Not in rain, and not with drops or mist on any camera dome: wait until they have dried. Do not wipe a dome; that can move the camera.
  2. Start the calibration recorder on the field PC, in a cmd window in the repository folder: powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File calibration_record.ps1. It records all 12 streams into a new E:\calibration\session_<date>_<time> folder, alongside the production recorders, and stops with Ctrl+C. Check that every stream is growing.
  3. NVR live view: all six ground cameras in colour, none switched to black-and-white IR.
  4. Clocks: every time on this sheet is PC time. Look at the PC clock and your phone at the same moment and write the difference, then add it to what your phone shows in the field.

4Record the new cone set

Everyone out of every camera view for 60 s, in daylight (colour, not IR). Check the NVR live view that no one is in frame, then write the PC clock time.

The ground is not flat, and that is fine

The model puts every camera onto one flat plane. Where the real ground is higher or lower by h, an oblique camera (CH01–CH04) reads the point about 0.9 h too far from or too close to itself; a shelter camera (CH05, CH06) only about 0.1 h. Two cameras looking from opposite sides are pushed in opposite directions, so a 5 cm bump can put them up to about 9 cm apart. Part of today's 6–7 cm median disagreement at the handoffs is probably this, not the calibration.

The cones stand on the real ground, grass and all, so every labelled cone carries its own local height. The ground correction is fitted to those cones, which puts every camera on the right spot at each cone whatever the bump; between cones it interpolates. That is why the cones are dense (27–36 in apart), and no height has to be measured for it to work. Where two cameras see the same cone, their two rays also give the height of the ground there, so the new set yields a coarse terrain map for free. Nothing about the ground is measured in the field.

5Optional: new cords

Cords are traced along their whole length in every camera, so a cord gives many more points than a cone. The two long cords matter most: every camera today has cords only across the paddock (they fix x), and only the side walls fix y. Do this after step 4 is recorded.

  1. Y39 and Y201 (along the length, x = 0 wall to x = 480 wall). Lift the seven cones on that line first; their positions are already on video. Tie the cord so it crosses every T cord exactly at the 39 in (or 201 in) mark: 27 in above tick 12, or 27 in above tick 174. Keep it taut and on the ground.
  2. X60 and X420 (across, lower value): 36 in inside cords T1 and T7, wall to wall, through the V/F1 and V/F6 cone positions.
  3. Everyone out of view 60 s again and write the time.

6Sweep a ball over the whole paddock

A ball of known size, moved slowly, stands in for a rat. In every frame where two cameras see it, the difference between their two readings is the handoff jump at a known height at that spot, and the time shift that lines up their two tracks is the delay between those two camera streams. Swept over the whole paddock, it gives that jump everywhere a rat can go, which is what the place-field analysis needs. The cones cannot measure either. About 25 minutes (10 of them walking), after step 4 (and 5) is recorded.

  • The ball: one plain colour that stands out on grass (orange, yellow or red; not white with black panels, not green), 15–25 cm across. Measure its circumference with the tape.
  • Clear the way: pick the cones up first; their positions are already on video.
  • Move it with a stick or on a 1–2 m string, so you stay a step away from it, at a slow, steady walk (about half your normal pace).
  • Write the PC time at the start and end of each block.

Free, 5 minutes: wander anywhere, change direction often, into the corners, around the shelters and along the walls.

Why lanes and not only wandering: a person walking at random covers the middle many times and the corners and walls, where rats spend much of their time, hardly at all. The lanes put every point of the paddock within 34 cm of the ball's path, pass through 100 % of every two-camera overlap and cross each camera's edge 12–20 times. Neighbouring lanes run in opposite directions and the free part adds every other direction; that spread of directions is what separates a camera delay (the ball always a little behind in that camera, whichever way it moves) from a position error (the same offset whichever way it moves).

After

  1. Stop the recorder with Ctrl+C in its window. It closes every segment itself (the file names get _to_HH-MM-SS).
  2. Copy the new session folder from E:\calibration to the calibration folder on the portable drive. If the ultrasound mics or the neurologger dongle are still in use, ask before a long copy over USB.
  3. Send Claude the times and notes from this page (they are stored only in this browser), the clock difference, and the ball's colour and circumference.
