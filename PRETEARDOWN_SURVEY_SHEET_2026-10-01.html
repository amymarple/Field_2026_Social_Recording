Pole, house and wall survey

What the calibration still needs from the field, in the order two independent reviews ranked it. Some of it needs the cameras recording, so that comes first. After the teardown none of it can be measured again.

About two hourstwo people; steps 1–5 need the cameras recording (about 45 min), the rest does not

What the measurements are for

  • The floor. The fit assumes one flat ground plane. At the far end the panoramas move a point 2.3–2.5 cm sideways for every 1 cm the real ground is higher or lower, and nothing measured so far tells how flat it is. Both reviews put this first.
  • Still targets at the CH04 end. On the moving sweep, CH04's clock and its position error look the same. A still ball or staff needs no clock.
  • IR and colour. Switching the IR filter shifts the CH03/CH04 images by 10–18 px (2–5 cm on the ground). Night footage needs that shift, measured with the scene still.
  • A clock event that every camera sees gives the cameras' relative timing to one frame.
  • Poles, houses, wall top are fixed structures at heights the cameras see. They are also the only way to calibrate cohorts 1–2 afterwards.

Compass words on this sheet: W = toward x = 0, E = toward x = 480, S = toward row A (y = 0), N = toward row C (y = 240). Camera chips = cameras that see that point.

Before going out

  1. Start the calibration recorder on the field PC (cmd, in the repository folder): powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File calibration_record.ps1. Check that every stream grows.
  2. All six ground cameras in colour on the NVR live view. Daylight; dry domes. One person stays able to switch the cameras' IR mode (step 4).
  3. Bring: laser rangefinder, 5 m tape, bright orange or red tape, a marker, a phone with a level app, the ball, a card to tape on a pole as a laser target, and:
    • a level reference: a clear hose 10 m or more filled with water (a water level), or a line laser on a tripod;
    • a straight staff about 1.5 m long with tape marks whose lower edge is at 0 (the tip), 60, 105, 200 and 300 mm, alternating colours.
  4. Pick the datum before starting: one fixed mark that stays put, e.g. a pencil line on pole B2 at about 50 cm. Every "vs datum" number on this sheet is the ground there minus the datum, in cm (negative = lower).
  5. Write the PC time of every recorded step. If anyone bumps a camera or its pole, write the time too.

  3. After the far end: push the ball slowly along cord T7 (x 456) from y 12 to y 228, stopping 3 s at every tick, then back from y 228 to y 12, stopping again.

2Staff at known heights cameras recording

At every spot tagged "staff here too", stand the staff on the ground with its 0 mark at the bottom, held vertical (phone level against it), for 8–10 s. It shows each camera points at 0, 60, 105, 200 and 300 mm above the same ground: the height scale near rat height, with no clock involved.

3One clock event cameras recording

  1. Everyone out of view except one person standing in the open near x 180, y 120 (between the west house and pole B2), where all six cameras see them. That person jumps once, clearly. Write the PC time to the second.
  2. Hold a phone showing a network clock with seconds (e.g. time.is) in front of CH05 for 10 s and write the PC time. This ties the PC clock to what the cameras burn into their picture.

4IR and colour, scene still cameras recording

  1. Everyone out of view, nothing moving in the paddock.
  2. Switch all six cameras to black-and-white (IR) for 60 s, then back to colour for 60 s. Do it twice.
  3. Write the PC time of each switch. The cameras must not be touched; switch them from the NVR or the app.

5Bands on the poles cameras recording

Only if step 8 (pole positions) will be done too; bands without positions are of no use. On each pole a camera sees, wrap two turns of tape with the top edge at about 120 cm and 200 cm above the ground at the foot, then everyone out of view for 30 s. The exact heights are measured in step 8.

Part 2 · no recording needed

6Floor heights against the datum

  1. Set up the level reference so it reaches the datum and as many spots as possible; move it as often as needed, re-reading the datum (or a point already read) after each move.
  2. At every still-ball spot (step 1 table), every pole foot (step 8 table), every house corner (step 7), the wall foot points (step 9) and below every camera (step 7): the height of the soil surface (grass pressed down) relative to the datum, in cm.
  3. Note roughly how tall the grass is where the ball sat at the far end.

7Cameras and houses

Lens height above the ground directly below it (laser straight up, or the tape), what it hangs on, its horizontal offset from that pole's face, and the ground there vs the datum. The fit's value is there to catch a slip. One review reads the fit 6–12 cm above the taped heights for CH03 and CH04; this measurement settles it.

8Poles

  • Circumference with the tape at about 120 cm, or the two widths if the pole is square (the images suggest about 14 cm across, a 6 x 6 in post; the base may be thicker).
  • Lean with the phone's level app held flat against the pole at about 150 cm: on the face toward +x (E) and on the face toward +y (N). Positive = the top leans that way.
  • Bands (if step 5 was done): the exact height of each band's top edge above the ground at the foot.
  • Distances: hold the rangefinder flat against pole P at the lower band and aim at the same height on pole Q, along the line between the centres (tape the card on Q if the beam misses). When done

  1. Stop the calibration recorder (Ctrl+C) and copy the session to the analysis PC before anything is taken down.
  2. Copy the entries below and paste them into the chat before leaving the field. They live only in this browser on this device; clearing it or switching phones loses them.
What happens with the numbers: the pole network gives each pole's centre to about 1 cm; the bands give points at two heights on each pole; the poles' edges, the house corners and the wall top enter the bundle (fit_cameras.py) as straight-line and 3-D constraints, the lens models are refitted, then the ground correction, and the three checks run again (boards, held-out cones, balls). The still-ball spots enter refit_supplement.py --balls as tie points that need no clock.
