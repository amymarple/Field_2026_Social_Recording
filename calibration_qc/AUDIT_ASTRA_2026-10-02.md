## 1. Error budget and the two cheapest discriminating experiments

**Verdict: confirmed that several systematic errors remain; unknown how much each contributes. Refuted that the present summaries uniquely allocate the residual to a camera-model ceiling.**

For camera pair a,b, the residual vector is approximately the difference between their spatial-model biases, plus their image-registration errors propagated through the pixel-to-ground Jacobians, plus height-sensitivity vectors times height errors, plus velocity times relative timing error, plus label errors. These terms are correlated: fitting the warp and clocks can absorb one another. A shared error in the physical coordinate frame can disappear from pairwise agreement entirely. Medians of vector magnitudes cannot be subtracted, or combined by root-sum-of-squares, to recover a variance budget. The board summary also uses the *maximum pair distance per corner*, whereas ball/cone summaries pool pair distances (`Q/paddock_agreement.py:69–99`; `Q/refit_supplement.py:234–259`).

## 2. Is the stitched panorama model the binding limit?

**Verdict: unknown. Confirmed that the model is approximate; refuted that the available evidence proves a single-centre ceiling near 7 cm.**

The implementation assigns one bearing direction per upright panorama pixel, using equirectangular angular scales (`Q/fit_models.py:34–38,63–69`). It intersects that ray with z and applies the same xy polynomial at every requested height (`Q/paddock_map.py:98–105`). It is therefore height-*parameterized*, but its empirical correction is not height-*validated*. A flexible ground map can reproduce one visible surface even when the underlying camera is non-central, provided the image-to-surface correspondence is locally unique. A cubic may be insufficient in some regions; that does not prove that two physical lens centres are the relevant limitation.

`Q/fit_cameras.py:62–64` says a split fit failed to improve residuals and concludes that the stitch is one projection centre. The conclusion is too strong. The implemented split assigns whole board views to L/R by median image x and handles seam-straddling views specially (`:77–84`); it is not recovery of the vendor's lens projection, seam blending and depth-dependent stitching. I did not verify the old split run's numerical results. Neither a failed simple split nor a successful smooth ground warp establishes centrality.

| Candidate | Recommendation, identifiability and expected gain | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Two physical lenses plus vendor stitch | **Defer.** Original lens images or a known stitch map would help greatly. Stitched output alone does not expose correspondence through blended seam pixels. Lens spacing, seam location and depth diversity are needed to distinguish separate centres from angular distortion. | Several days or more; **no defensible numerical gain** now. Do not budget a 100–200 mm rescue. |
| Small regularized angular residual field | **Best model experiment if A leaves reproducible curvature.** Fit few smooth angular corrections, with seam sides treated separately if a discontinuity is observed. Hold out whole poles and regions. Avoid an unconstrained per-pixel map. | Several days; planning range **10–30 mm typical**, **20–60 mm local** after the other errors are controlled; possibly zero. |
| Height-dependent residual correction | **Potentially useful at rat height**, but ground/50/105 mm observations are mixed across different objects, dates and positions. They do not independently identify a height derivative. Record the same identifiable target at the same xy and several known heights. | 10–20 additional field minutes at selected spots, then 1–3 desk days; target **20–50 mm local** improvement if current height derivatives are wrong. Unvalidated beyond sampled heights. |

The existing pole labels constrain straightness in bearing space, and verticality only to the extent that poles are actually plumb. They constrain little about position *along* each line. Measured pole positions, lean, band heights, house corners and wall geometry add metric 3-D information, but sparse nearly vertical lines still do not identify a dense non-central ray field. High bands alone give poor coverage of pixels that image rats near the floor. Add low, identifiable targets at approximately **0, 60, 105 and 200–300 mm**, distributed near the seam, panorama ends and overlaps; a rigid marked vertical staff is preferable to guessing a projected sphere centre.

The published pole check is also **mode-confounded**: `Q/plumb_check.py:9–23` directly feeds IR landmark pixels into colour-calibrated rays; `Q/pole_check.py:15–25` does the same. The dramatic CH03 corner tilts and derived pole widths/positions are warning signals, not calibrated surveys. Pano IR shifts are smaller, so their long-line curvature is more persuasive, but still does not quantify a 2-D floor error. Pole lean or physical curvature must be distinguished from optical curvature.

This preference for modest, spatially supported flexibility is consistent with generic camera models representing observation directions, or full observation lines for non-central cameras; it is not evidence that this dataset can identify thousands of parameters. See the primary research [Why Having 10,000 Parameters in Your Camera Model Is Better Than Twelve](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Why-Having-10000-Parameters-in-Your-Camera-Model-is-Better-Than-Twelve.pdf).

## 3. Epoch and colour/IR coordinate chains

**Verdict: confirmed algebraically correct for the supplement; unknown empirical accuracy of all links. Refuted that landmark_track's existing status alone certifies a usable colour-calibration chain.**

The saved similarity is forward **09-18 colour→09-30 colour**: `u30 = c + s R (u18−c) + d`. `Q/refit_supplement.py:78–86` correctly inverts it, using row vectors: `u18 = c + (u30−c−d) R / s`. Cones, cords and accepted ball centres all pass through it (`:93–116`); unchecked machine marks are excluded. CH05/06 default to identity, consistent with the supplied static-match evidence, but identity is an assumption without its own uncertainty propagation. The transform measured at 16:35:10 is also used for balls at 16:22:40–16:31:20. Within-session stability during that interval remains unverified. No additional IR correction should be inserted into this already colour→colour route.

For cohort footage, let **I** be a camera's 09-18 IR reference coordinates, **C** its 09-18 colour-calibration coordinates, **T(t)** the target frame coordinates, and **B** the measured I→C bridge. Let **F(t)** be landmark_track's *forward* I→T(t) affine, represented homogeneously. Then:

`raw/stored pixel → upright pixel uT → inverse(F(t)) uT → B inverse(F(t)) uT → to_paddock(z = ground elevation + keypoint height)`.

For an alternate cohort reference S, the tracker computes `F(t) = A_track(t) A_tie`, where `A_tie` maps I→S (`A/cv/cv_field/landmark_track.py:41–46,791–817`). Consequently the correction is `B inverse(A_tie) inverse(A_track)`. Invert the full affine, not merely tx/ty; do not apply its reported forward displacement as though it were the correction. Day and night use this same expression **if F is actually estimated to the IR reference**. The cross-mode shift is already part of F for day frames; adding another target-mode offset on top would double-correct it. Alternatively, maintain independently bridged mode-specific references and document their composition explicitly.

Measure B independently per camera, ideally from paired same-pose captures immediately around an IR-cut switch, repeated to test repeatability/hysteresis. Use rigid corners, nails and verified pole centrelines across the frame, checking spatially varying residuals and IR bloom. Start with a translation, increase to affine only if held-out structure requires it. Verify all six cameras; small pano offsets are not automatically negligible at the far end. Record resolution, rotation, mode, focus/zoom, settings and switch time. Do not infer a subpixel B from unrelated day/night shadows or a pole's changing apparent width.

Two implementation qualifications matter:

- The present tracker measures a forward affine but does not persist its full per-frame matrix in `track_frames.csv`; it writes centre shifts, mean rotation and column norms (`A/cv/cv_field/landmark_track.py:817–824,847–851`). Those summaries do not uniquely retain affine shear. The alternate-reference tie matrix is saved separately in `tie.json` (`:897–900`), but that does not recover the missing per-frame matrix. Save the full per-frame A and associate it with the tie, mode bridge, provenance and confidence for downstream correction. This audit did not change that code.
- An alternate-reference tie can be `unreliable` yet still have a non-null A and be composed. The final per-frame status is the tracking status, not the conjunction of all link statuses (`:791–797,815–824`). The saved CH04 night-reference report explicitly has an **unreliable tie, 3.05/3.75 px**, while **29/42** tracked frames are marked ok; day-reference tie is **1.89/4.79 px**, with **12/14** frames ok (`A/results/2026c/cv_field/reports/cv_field_landmark_track_2026c_ch04_ref0904night.md:3`, day counterpart `:3`). Gate the whole chain and propagate both tie and tracking error. Median pixel error alone is inadequate at sensitive ground locations.

Expected gain: **tens of mm** from fixing a missing mode bridge, and avoiding much larger errors from the wrong transform direction or an unreliable tie. Cost: **2–4 desk hours** to make the chain explicit and validate a few frames, plus capture time if no adequate existing mode pairs exist. I inspected code and saved reports, not raw frame overlays; I cannot certify the visual matches or all historical camera epochs.

## 4. Evidence quality and corrected statistical interpretation

**Verdict: confirmed that the release improves several diagnostics; refuted that all three checks are independent end-to-end validation. Unknown selection-adjusted generalization error.**

**Boards.** They are absent from the warp objective but present in the bundle and used during method selection. The agreement check also removes five views rejected by the fit (`Q/paddock_agreement.py:43–49`). It measures consistency on accepted training placements, not unseen placement accuracy. Preserve the exclusions, but report their count and coverage rather than treating the survivors as the entire field. CH02–CH03's board median comes from **one placement**, CH01–CH02 from **four**, and CH01–CH04 from **five** (`Q/PADDOCK_AGREEMENT.txt`). Hundreds of corners cannot replace those sample sizes.

I reweighted the **31 station/session rows already printed in that report**, giving each row one vote. Their median disagreement is **50 mm**, p90 **99 mm**, versus corner-weighted 52/108. A 20,000-resample percentile bootstrap of those 31 rounded summaries, seed 20261002, gives **44–68 mm** for the median. Definition: each row summarizes maximum camera-pair disagreement per shared corner, then its median; this is not the median of pooled camera pairs. The report groups by station/session rather than full placement window (`Q/paddock_agreement.py:104–109`), so this is a **station/session-cluster proxy**, not a reconstructed exact placement bootstrap. The interval is conditional on the fitted release, accepted sites and exchangeability of these clusters. Spatial correlation, selection, calibration uncertainty and model bias are not included; it is not a 95% interval on absolute rat-position accuracy. It is nevertheless a more honest sample-size statement than treating 2,075 corners as independent.

**Cones.** Holding out whole IDs across cameras is substantially better than holding out individual marks (`Q/refit_supplement.py:287–296`). But the split is random across space, all cords and balls remain, and the old 09-18 lattice remains. Unmoved IDs such as T15–T75 can still have their old observations in the base fit. Thus **61/119 mm** supports local interpolation of withheld supplement observations, not an unseen region, a fresh layout or absolute metric truth. Shared-camera pairs for a cone are correlated. Next evaluation should hold out a contiguous spatial patch/cord group across all relevant sources; choose weight/degree within training regions, then score the untouched patch.

**Balls.** There are three distinct issues, beyond correlation within a pass:

1. `ball_velocity()` uses all accepted marks and adjacent times (`Q/refit_supplement.py:120–136`), and V0 is computed once before any split (`:266`). Fold fitting and scoring both use that V0 (`:315–318`). Training velocities at boundaries can depend on test marks; test corrections also use velocities estimated from test observations. Calling this completely held-out is inaccurate. The bias has no guaranteed direction. Camera coverage changes can also perturb the averaged trajectory, and `position + tau*velocity` is only a first-order timing approximation around bends/accelerations.
2. Reconstructing only the input filtering and block membership, without fitting, recovered **331 usable marks, 100 multi-camera times and five blocks of 20 times**, exactly matching the saved run. Block k ranges are **0–19, 20–50, 51–91, 92–213, 218–260**. They are blocks of observed multi-camera times, not equal-duration segments or necessarily disjoint spatial regions. **All 13 usable CH04 marks are in block 5; its training folds contain zero CH04 ball constraints when block 5 is held out.** Its tau is then driven to zero by the prior (`:209–212`), not learned from that fold. This explains why the saved held-out CH01–CH04/CH02–CH04 medians are **428/345 mm**, compared with in-sample **201/293 mm**. It does not prove that CH04's pure spatial error is 428 mm.
3. Ball weight 0.5 was chosen using these same checks. The **88 versus 89 mm** held-out difference from adding balls is not demonstrated improvement; it is below the resolution supported by one correlated sweep and this selection process. The weight trials already span **88–90 mm**. Selection bias is a general issue, not something a bootstrap of the selected pooled residuals can fix; see [Cawley and Talbot, 2010](https://www.jmlr.org/beta/papers/v11/cawley10a.html).

Keep the published 88 mm as a **selected, partially data-dependent diagnostic**, with its **260 mm p90** and regional failures, not an unbiased performance estimate. The saved run contains only `camera_fit.npz`, `frame_correction.json`, `PADDOCK_AGREEMENT.txt` and `REFIT_SUPPLEMENT.txt`; it does not retain per-fold prediction arrays/coefficients needed to calculate a genuine segment-level confidence interval without refitting. I therefore do **not** invent a corrected ball CI. Five overlapping-training folds from one sweep are not five independent experiments.

A clean future test freezes the chosen model and mode/epoch transforms, uses new still-target placements for spatial accuracy, and separate complete motion traversals for clock generalization. For reuse of existing data, withhold whole traversals/regions with temporal guard gaps, derive training velocities from training data only, and score independently timed/interpolated tracks with explicit uncertainty. If withholding a traversal removes all timing information for a camera, report that camera as **clock-unidentified**, or supply an independent clock estimate; do not silently interpret its zero-prior result as geometric accuracy. Bootstrap whole placements/traversals, preserving paired comparisons between models.

Cost: the aggregation above took seconds; **1–2 desk hours** to specify/persist proper grouped predictions, more if new fits are later authorized. Expected geometric gain **0 mm from better reporting itself**; its benefit is avoiding an unjustified model choice. A reproduction caveat: `Q/refit_supplement.py:65,263–266` loads the adjacent current warp for the baseline and V0. After promotion that is the new release, so the old evaluation baseline is not reproduced by a bare rerun of the release command. Pin the September 24 warp when recreating the historical comparison. I did not rerun it.

For a leaning pole, heights measured along its face and points at the lower band are not its ground-foot centre. At 2 m, 1° of lean is about **35 mm**, already large enough to matter.

Minimum sequence if time runs out: **far-end stationary spots → mode bridge → common-datum elevations and several measured low-height targets → visible-pole controls/camera offsets → opposite-direction pass → houses → remaining wall/network density**. Several activities can share setup. Save raw frames, per-spot times, sketches, uncertainty and duplicate measurement checks; export the sheet rather than leaving the only copy in browser storage. Preserve a few whole spots/poles/regions for validation. Do not put every new measurement into the fit and then call it validation.

The existing teardown checklist also covers UWB dwells and shelter-interior geometry (`R/PRE_TEARDOWN_CAPTURE_CHECKLIST.md:29–54`). Those have independent scientific purposes; low priority for this camera handoff question is not a recommendation to omit them from the broader study.

## 6. Tracking rules that reduce handoff jumps

**Verdict: confirmed that a consistent height/time/epoch convention and quality-aware camera use have more immediate value than another global fit. Unknown final place-field robustness until tested on actual trajectories.**

1. **Define one anatomical keypoint and height convention.** Use a reproducible dorsal torso keypoint for floor locomotion if that is what the detector can observe, with an initial **60 mm above local ground** convention; treat that number as an explicit hypothesis to test over, for example, 40–80 mm. Never mix back centroids, nose, bounding-box bottom and feet as though they were the same physical point. A silhouette or box centre is view-dependent, so simultaneous boxes are not automatically 3-D correspondences. Flag rearing, climbing and roof occupancy; use the appropriate plane or exclude those samples from floor place fields. This can remove **138–150 mm** of far-end pano displacement compared with mapping a 60 mm-high back at z=0. Remaining ±20 mm uncertainty still means about **46–50 mm per pano** there. Cost: <1 hour to specify, then targeted keypoint/posture validation.

2. **Correct geometry and timing before overlap fusion.** Apply the validated epoch/mode chain and map all detections to one physical convention and common timestamp. Select a primary camera using local empirical residuals, visibility, height sensitivity, occlusion and support—not detector confidence alone. Favor a nadir view where it is validated and unobstructed. Use hysteresis to prevent rapid camera switching. The convex support hull is a numerical extrapolation guard, not proof of uniform accuracy: the release hull includes every accepted ball mark, including single-camera marks (`Q/refit_supplement.py:359–366`), and can bridge poorly sampled space. Maintain local validation masks, especially for x>400 in.

3. **Blend only mutually consistent, same-animal detections.** In a trusted overlap, use slowly varying quality weights; evaluate both tracks at the same time. If their disagreement exceeds the locally validated envelope, retain the reliable primary camera and flag the interval instead of averaging away a diagnostic failure. Blending can remove a discrete **50–100 mm** switch step where the underlying disagreement is of that size, but it does not remove bias. With position `p=w p1+(1−w)p2`, changing weight creates a velocity term `(p1−p2) dw/dt`; an overly quick blend can manufacture movement and occupancy. CH04's 200+ mm mismatch should not be hidden by a long smooth transition. Cost: 1–2 implementation days plus trajectory checks.

4. **Use simultaneous rats as secondary checks or carefully constrained tie points.** Require verified identity, the same anatomical landmark, reliable clocks, matching posture and no occlusion. Start with stationary/quasi-stationary floor animals to suppress timing confounding, then inspect moving crossings in both directions. Such data can refine small residual offsets with strong priors, but cannot define absolute scale or independently validate a calibration they trained. Hold out whole animals/time bouts/days and preserve the physical-target calibration as the anchor. Shared pose and detector biases can otherwise make the cameras agree for the wrong reason. Potential gain **10–30 mm** after other corrections; days of engineering/curation, lower priority than static targets now.

5. **Estimate clocks per continuous recording segment, with daily structure only where supported.** The ball's CH04 +0.82 s is a parameter of its calibration recording, partly confounded with position; it is not a universal camera delay. Use archived common LED pulses/logs where available, or robust matched events with diverse velocity directions. Treat recorder restarts and hourly filename boundaries explicitly; retain piecewise offsets, uncertainty and a drift term only if observed. Align video to the ephys timebase as well as camera to camera. A **20 mm timing allowance at 0.42 m/s requires about 48 ms** relative timing uncertainty, much less than one-second filename precision. If that cannot be established, favor static/slow validation and avoid fusing asynchronous fast detections. Potential gain **20–100+ mm**; 1–3 desk hours to assess existing sync coverage, potentially days for a complete historical estimator.

For place fields, apply exactly the same position/time convention to occupancy and spike assignment. Report camera, mode, epoch, keypoint/posture, support and uncertainty with positions. Validate robustness by repeating the actual analysis under plausible height/bridge/clock perturbations and excluding weak handoffs, at both 5 and 10 cm bins. A 10 cm bin is not an accuracy certificate, and better camera agreement cannot reveal a common spatial distortion. The stale ensemble sections in `Q/DOWNSTREAM_VALIDATION.txt` do not answer this question for the new release (`Q/RELEASE_2026-10-01.md:74–79`).

## Do not do

- Do not spend teardown time collecting another dense set of nominal-grid cones without independent xy/z control, distinct heights or genuinely new handoff coverage.
- Do not declare a two-lens/single-centre verdict from ground residuals, unbridged IR pole labels or the old simplistic split test.
- Do not free every board's height without a constrained ground model and independent height information; the previous sinking-board failure is documented in `Q/fit_cameras.py:54–60`.
- Do not increase polynomial degree, fit a dense ray field, or optimize more SAM centres before resolving reference modes, datum definitions and CH04 timing/spatial identifiability.
- Do not reuse the rejected ball-outline refinement, assume every accepted machine centre is unbiased, or mistake a projected sphere ellipse centre for an independently known 3-D centre.
- Do not report 88 versus 89 mm as proven ball-term improvement, bootstrap individual corners/marks as independent, or present the 44–68 mm conditional board interval as absolute animal accuracy.
- Do not rerun expensive pipelines merely to regenerate established numbers. The useful next work is the two controlled comparisons and the targeted irreversible field capture above.
- Do not assume this audit verified raw image matches, board tilt truth, survey precision, historical mode offsets, absolute floor accuracy or a final model gain. It verified code paths, release hashes, saved evidence and the bounded calculations explicitly identified here; those other quantities remain unverified.
